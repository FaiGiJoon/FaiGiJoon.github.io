Helping people through technology

I'm an IT Operations Specialist with a background in network management, system administration, IT support and cybersecurity. In my latest role I was responsible for the network infrastructure, camera systems, servers and workstations of a security company with around 8,000 monitored locations, from configuration and rollout to fixing outages remotely on my own.

Before that I supported employees at global companies like FedEx and Prosus, and taught networks, servers and cybersecurity to MBO Level 4 students at ROC van Amsterdam.

My interest in tech started with Pokémon Platinum and the DeSmuME emulator. Figuring out how an emulator worked was my lightbulb moment, and by 13 I was hooked on computers. In 2015 I did my first IT internship, building and refurbishing PCs and setting them up with the brand-new Windows 10. Seeing someone's "slow" computer feel new again is what made me want to help people through technology.

Long term, I want to build automation software and new technology that helps hospitals around the world.

More about meShow less

On LinkedIn I describe myself as a creative thinker who blends technical skill with empathy, always looking to help others and improve the world around me. That is still the shortest honest version.

I've been into programming, networking and security since I was 16, and most of what I know I taught myself by building things: first PCs, then networks, then my own tools. School gave me the foundations; the home lab is where it stuck.

The work I enjoy most starts where the routine stops: an outage nobody can explain, a device that refuses to authenticate, a task repeated so often it should have been a script. That's why I'm moving towards network engineering, systems and security work with room to build and automate, not only maintain. Healthcare is where I'd most like that work to end up.

I care about people looking out for each other. "We're all in this together" is how I try to work: sharing what I know, onboarding new colleagues properly, and writing things down so the next person isn't stuck.

Away from the screen I walk my dog every day, and I go to the gym, run, cycle and hike.

PokeSyncBacks up emulator save files so they can move between PCs

One of my first tools that really worked, written in Python. It finds games across emulators (GameCube, Wii, 3DS and older systems) and backs up their saves with metadata, so a lost save can be restored.
